The Skyscraper That Could Have Fallen in a Storm, and the Secret Night Repairs (Citicorp Center, 1978)

A 59-storey tower in Manhattan standing on four columns in the middle of its sides, not its corners, because of a church. In 1978 a student asked what happens when the wind hits it diagonally. The answer: the braces carried 40 percent more than designed, the welded joints had been built as bolted ones, and a storm expected once every sixteen years could bring it down.

The video follows the engineer's calculation, the tuned mass damper that bought a little margin, and the secret night-time welding that fixed the building before anyone outside knew.

In this video

  • Why mid-face columns change the worst wind direction
  • Chevron bracing and load paths
  • Quartering winds and the 40 percent
  • Bolted versus welded joints
  • Return periods: 16, 55 and 700 years
  • What a tuned mass damper does
