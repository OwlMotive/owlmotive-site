The Brachistochrone: Why the Fastest Path Isn't a Straight Line

Which ramp gets a bead from A to B fastest? Not the straight one. The fastest path dips steeply first to gain speed, then flattens: a cycloid. This is the brachistochrone, a problem that started the calculus of variations.

The video races the straight line, a circular arc and the cycloid, shows the surprising tautochrone property, and connects it to Snell's law of light.

In this video

  • Why the straight line loses
  • The cycloid as the fastest curve
  • Tautochrone: same arrival time from any start
  • The light-ray analogy and Snell's law
