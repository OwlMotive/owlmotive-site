Two Claimed Proofs in One Day: The Riemann Hypothesis Explained

Up close the prime numbers look random, yet counted in bulk they follow a smooth curve, and Bernhard Riemann showed in 1859 that the error is made of waves, one for every zero of the zeta function. This deep dive builds the staircase of primes, rebuilds it from 100 zeros, and explains the Riemann hypothesis (every zero on one line), why 12 trillion checked zeros do not prove it, why it is so hard, and what a proof would and would not change.

On 20.11.2026 two proofs were announced within hours of each other: one by Levent Alpöge, found working with the AI system Claude and released as Lean code, and a human-only proof by Alessandro Masullo, announced by Ravi Vakil. As of 20.11.2026 the Lean code has passed a first independent computer check, experts are still confirming that it states exactly the right problem, and neither claim has been accepted by mathematicians. Until a proof is accepted, the problem officially stays open.
