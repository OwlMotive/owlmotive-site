Она была права, когда 1000 докторов наук твердили, что она ошибается (парадокс Монти Холла)

In 1990 a magazine columnist told her readers to switch doors on a game show, and about ten thousand letters, hundreds from PhDs, told her she was wrong. She was right. This is the Monty Hall problem: three doors, a car, two goats, and a host who knows where the car is.

The video tells the story, then shows the maths four ways: the three cases, the hundred-door version, the full grid of games, a real simulation of ten thousand rounds, and Bayes' rule in one line. It ends with the variations where the answer changes, and why.

  • The 100-door version
