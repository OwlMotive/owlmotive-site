Magnetars Explained: How a Dead Star Becomes the Strongest Magnet in the Universe

On 27 December 2004 a flash of gamma rays from the far side of the galaxy blinded satellites and disturbed Earth's upper atmosphere. It lasted two tenths of a second and came from a star smaller than a city. This is the full story of magnetars: how a collapsing star makes the strongest magnetic field in the universe, how we measure that field from fifty thousand light-years away, and what happens when it breaks the star's crust.

Every number on screen is computed: the field from the spin-down, the power that rotation cannot pay for, the energy stored in empty space.
