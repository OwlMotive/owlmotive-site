Pulsars Explained: Lighthouses, Clocks, and the Proof of Gravitational Waves

A bit of scruff on a paper chart in 1967 turned out to be a new kind of star. This is the full story of pulsars: why only a neutron star can spin that fast, how a spinning magnet becomes a lighthouse, and how the Crab Nebula is lit by a flywheel.

Then what we do with them: the first planets found outside the solar system, the binary whose shrinking orbit gave the first evidence of gravitational waves, a detector the size of the galaxy, navigation for spacecraft, and the pulsar map on the Pioneer and Voyager probes.
