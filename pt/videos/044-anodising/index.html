Why Aluminium Doesn't Rust Away (and What Anodising Really Does)

Aluminium gives up its electrons far more easily than iron, so it should corrode away first. It does not, because its oxide is a hard, tight skin a few nanometres thick that seals the metal. Anodising grows that skin thousands of times thicker, as a honeycomb of pores that can take dye and be sealed shut.
